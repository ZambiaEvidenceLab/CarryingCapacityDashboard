# Invert population-pressure indicators within the Environment sector

Environment mixes ecological-condition indicators (forest cover, ecological diversity) with population-pressure indicators (population growth rate, cattle-head per capita, charcoal consumption). The general rule across the CCA is that every indicator is oriented so higher = more capacity. For Environment, "more capacity" means more room to sustain the population sustainably — so the pressure indicators are inverted (higher population growth, higher cattle-head per capita, or higher charcoal consumption pulls the Environment index *down*), while the condition indicators are left oriented as-is (higher forest cover or ecological diversity pulls the index *up*).

This follows directly from the README's own framing of Environment as tracking "ecological conditions **and population pressures** that shape carrying capacity" — treating a pressure indicator as a positive contribution would contradict that framing and produce a nonsensical index (a district under severe environmental strain scoring *higher* because the strain indicators are large numbers).
