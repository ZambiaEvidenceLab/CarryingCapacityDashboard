# Using the Carrying Capacity Assessment Dashboard

*A quick guide for Ministry of Finance planners*

The dashboard answers one question per sector: **which districts are most
underserved, and is the gap on the Supply side or the Access side?** Use it to
scan the country, rank priorities, and trace a low score to the specific
indicators behind it. Work top to bottom: **choose → scan → rank → drill →
decompose.**

---

## 1. Choose what you're looking at

Pick a **Sector** (Health, Education, Agriculture, Infrastructure, Environment)
and a **Measure** — *Overall*, *Supply*, or *Access*.

> 📷 *[Screenshot: the Sector selector, the Overall · Supply · Access control,
> and the subtitle beneath the title]*

**Draw attention to:**

- The **Measure toggle** — switching it re-frames the whole map and ranking as a
  supply-gap or access-gap question.
- The **"ⓘ what's in Supply / Access?"** hover — it lists which indicators make
  up each dimension, without opening a district.
- The **subtitle**, which always states the current view and the colour
  direction.
- For **Environment** the Measure toggle disables — it has no Supply/Access
  split.

---

## 2. Scan the map

The map shades all 116 districts by the selected score.

> 📷 *[Screenshot: the choropleth map, with the colour legend visible]*

**Draw attention to:**

- **Dark = more capacity; pale = more underserved.** This is the opposite of a
  "hotspot" map, and it is deliberate — this is a *capacity* index.
- Each sector has its **own colour** (Health red, Education blue, etc.) — a
  wayfinding cue when you switch sectors.
- Colour is **scaled to this sector's own range**, so the full palette is used
  even though scores cluster in the middle.
- **Amber dots** mark districts scored from **incomplete data** — treat a result
  there with caution.
- Look for **geographic clustering** of the pale districts — it tells you whether
  need is concentrated in a region.

---

## 3. Read the ranking

The list on the right is the actual priority answer: **districts sorted
worst-served first.**

> 📷 *[Screenshot: the ranked district list]*

**Draw attention to:**

- **Rank 1 is the most underserved** district for this sector and measure.
- The **microbar** makes each score scannable without reading numbers.
- The **amber completeness dot** and **"Urban" chip** repeat the map's caveats
  row by row.
- Clicking a row highlights the same district on the map (and vice versa) — the
  list and map stay in step.

---

## 4. Drill into a district

Click a district — on the map or in the list — to open its detail panel.

> 📷 *[Screenshot: the drawer open, showing the radar chart]*

**Draw attention to:**

- The **radar chart** compares this district's five Sector Index scores against
  the **national average** — its cross-sector strengths and weaknesses at a
  glance.
- The scan stays put underneath; closing the panel returns you exactly where you
  were.

---

## 5. Decompose a score

Below the radar, the **Decomposition View** opens automatically for the sector
you were viewing — no extra click. Click a radar axis to switch sectors.

> 📷 *[Screenshot: the decomposition cards, ideally one showing the
> compare-to-others chart]*

**Draw attention to:**

- Each indicator shows its **actual figure and unit** (e.g. "12.3 per 10k"), not
  just the 0–100 score.
- The **compare-to-others chart** places this district against all reporting
  districts — the highlighted dot is *this* district, the line is the **national
  average**, and where present, the amber line is the **National Development Plan
  objective**.
- Each indicator shows its **reference year and data source** — figures within
  one sector may come from different reporting cycles.
- An **"Incomplete data" alert** appears when the score was computed from missing
  indicators.
- For **urban districts under Agriculture**, a note explains that a low score
  reflects land use, not a capacity failure.

---

## Understanding the scores

- Scores are **relative**: each indicator is normalised across the 116 districts,
  so a score says "compared with other districts," not "against a fixed target."
- **The national average is not 50** — normalisation only pins the lowest and
  highest districts, and averaging indicators pulls scores toward the middle.
- The five Sector Indices are **never combined** into a single overall score — a
  district is strong in some sectors and weak in others, and that is the point.
- Full detail is behind the **Methodology** link in the header.
